# NUCLEUS VR development checkpoint

## Implemented

| Area | Files and behavior |
| --- | --- |
| Unity project | `ProjectSettings/ProjectVersion.txt` pins Unity 6000.6.10f1. `Packages/manifest.json` pins URP, OpenXR, Input System, Netcode for GameObjects, and Unity Transport. `Assets/NucleusVR/Scenes/Boot/Boot.unity` references the palette and direct-player prefab. `Editor/NucleusProjectSetup.cs` configures Quest features and validates Android builds. |
| Core VR | `Scripts/VR/` samples floor-relative head and controller poses. `Scripts/Movement/` resolves tracked hands against surfaces, drives the player capsule at fixed physics ticks, handles tracking loss and room-scale head obstruction, and caps movement and release speeds. Surface settings provide configurable hand slip and spatial contact taps. Snap turning is the default and smooth turning can be enabled. |
| Presentation | `Scripts/Maps/` builds an original facility calibration course, with a timed checkpoint route, physical reset and turn controls, and equipment cases with bounded spring grabs. `Scripts/Player/` builds and poses an original skinned gorilla with tracked elbows and wrists, two animated fingers and an opposable thumb per hand, and a moving 3D jaw. `Scripts/UI/` contains desktop controls and the physical VR session terminal. |
| Multiplayer foundation | `Scripts/Multiplayer/` and `Prefabs/Players/Direct Player.prefab` integrate online Quick Join, private rooms, room-code joins, Unity Relay transport, connection limits, sender-associated pose submissions, server validation, remote interpolation, retained spawn snapshots, and disconnect handling. Direct connections remain available to the loopback tests. The jaw expression path is available for a future voice provider. |
| Anatomy lab | `Scripts/Anatomy/` and `Scripts/Maps/AnatomyBay.cs` add two compatible specimens. Their six regions progress through wound, exposure, fracture, and severance states. Seven organ models have independent damage and extraction states. Host-owned ragdolls, grabs, throws, and anatomy snapshots are integrated with direct sessions. `Scripts/Gore/` supplies four local detail levels, pooled particles, surface stains, drips, bone chips, and spatial audio. |
| Normal Mode | `ResearchFacility` builds six connected zones, climb routes, and 18 interactive sites. Five original creatures use different sight, sound, pursuit, attack, and recovery behavior on a shared route graph. The facility cycles blackout, containment alerts, and radio transmissions. `FacilitySecrets` validates eight discoveries and multi-stage access puzzles. Individual discoveries persist on the device and room puzzles synchronize through the host. |
| Be the Monster | `MonsterRound` provides map voting, rotating monster selection, timed rounds, and late-join, capture, and escape states. The original Backrooms, Factory, and Mines arenas each require three objectives and have gated exits. The monster has stronger hand locomotion, strikes, two-hand carrying, and throwing. Offline practice covers the survivor routes. The Boot scene references a ScriptableObject for timers and map event settings. |
| Sandbox | `SandboxWorkshop` lets the host spawn two anatomy specimens and practice props, switch gravity and idle motion, and reset the room. Local gore quality has four levels. Dynamic specimens synchronize through the existing anatomy channel and request a baseline when activated. |
| Shared gameplay | `GameFlow` wires physical mode selection, role changes, spawning, and Quest status text into Boot. The host validates control reach from accepted tracked hands. Reliable snapshots establish mode, roles, and progression. Unreliable snapshots update AI and carrying between baselines. Host-authorized transfers invalidate stale hands until the player reaches the destination. |

The `Scripts/`, `Prefabs/`, and `Editor/` paths in the table are under `Assets/NucleusVR/`.

The uploaded [GorillaLoco+](https://github.com/developer9998/GorillaLocoPlus) Unity 2022 sample served as a feature reference for the adjacent grip and slip plates. NUCLEUS VR implements both behaviors in its own motor and generates the tap audio locally.

## Validation

- The offline Roslyn pass parses project C# under Unity Editor and Android symbol profiles. It checks language syntax and does not load Unity assemblies or run Unity's compiler pipeline.
- The static project verifier checks serialized references and object IDs, stable metadata, the enabled boot scene, the render pipeline link, and the collision layer assignments.
- A cloud-only offline .NET harness passed 12 checks. Six exercise production calibration progression and timing directly. Six exercise production pose validation using substitute Unity math and serializer declarations. Separate CPU checks covered mesh winding and elbow geometry.
- Managed harnesses with Unity test doubles checked tangential slide, retained push force, edge release, tap debouncing, and audio clip routing and cleanup. Unity physics and spatial playback still require Play Mode validation.
- Eight standalone C# anatomy state checks passed. A managed geometry probe checked 49 generated mesh variants, all region stages and detail levels, organ variants, and branching anatomy. The Unity anatomy physics, boot, and LAN regression tests still need the Editor.
- Ten standalone hunt rule checks passed, covering voting, rotation across 10 players, ordered objectives, capture, escape, timeout, disconnect, late join, practice, and roster reset. The gameplay snapshot codec rejected every truncated length and preserved the full ten-player state in a managed round trip.
- Four facility puzzle tests passed. A managed geometry probe compiled the facility and verified 18 station IDs, floor continuity and clearance for 14 patrol links, and the gates against the puzzle state. Static Backrooms checks verified the connected maze and isolated locked exit cells.
- Nine standalone pose and warp checks passed with substitute math and serialization types. New Unity Play Mode tests cover mode transitions, retained gameplay state, interaction reach, warps, roster changes, and dynamic anatomy baseline delivery.
- Unity Edit Mode and Play Mode suites are present for calibration, avatar geometry and posing, hand locomotion, boot wiring, pose validation, and two-endpoint loopback. Unity execution remains unverified. The cloud instance had no Unity Editor or license, and requests to the official Editor download and package registry received proxy 403 responses.
- The private Quest pipeline staged v0.1.0, v0.1.1, v0.1.2, and the current v0.1.3 build inputs with their Android version codes. The actual build command stopped because the activated Unity 6000.0.72f1 Editor and Android Build Support were unavailable. OpenXR startup, actual headset tracking, LAN interoperability on separate devices, comfort, and the 72 FPS Quest 2 target remain unverified.

## Resume from here

1. Open the project in Unity 6000.6.10f1 with Android Build Support. Run **Nucleus VR > Configure Project**, open the Boot scene, and run both Test Runner suites. Fix any Unity import, package, shader, or runtime failures, then rerun the affected tests.
2. Play through all four pads on desktop. Check that reaching below a low obstacle cannot send the camera through it, a fast hand sweep does not tunnel through walls, a released hand maintains bounded momentum, and a dropped case collides with the bay.
3. Install the same Android build on a Quest 2, 3, or 3S. Check floor-origin acquisition, left and right hand tracking, tracking loss and recentering, crouch fit, snap pivot, smooth turning, floor push, wall climbing, checkpoint reach, and equipment release. Repeat the loop on Quest 2 at 72 Hz and collect CPU and GPU frame timing, dropped frames, and memory with the platform profiler. Only enable higher refresh rates after profiling.
4. Host and join from separate devices. Check late join, room-full rejection, reconnect, pose jitter under packet loss, and peer removal. Verify the calibration bay remains playable during network failures.
5. Link a Unity Cloud project and validate the online flow across two devices on separate networks. Check public room discovery, private room code joins, full rooms, stale codes, and host departure. Expand network object ownership, cosmetics, persistence, and proximity voice after the online flow passes.
6. Validate the anatomy lab in Unity and on Quest. Verify that local and LAN strikes expose the same regions, a remote player can extract and throw an organ, a late joiner sees detached parts, and resets release held bodies. Profile the lab with both specimens damaged at High. Add the shared anatomy rig to live player and monster actors when combat roles are integrated.
7. Play through Normal Mode with two peers. Verify all six routes and eight discoveries, sound-sensitive patrols, event visibility, boss access and completion, and late-join puzzle state. Play each hunt arena with two or more peers and verify each exit, carry/release under network delay, late-join briefing, and recovery after a host leaves. Verify Sandbox changes, anatomy reacquisition, and prop resets from both endpoints.
8. Expand the facility monsters and hunt actors to live region damage and ragdolls, implement moving and breakable arena hazards, and add shared workshop prop movement and proximity voice. Profile headset lighting, geometry, physics, and network load before release.
